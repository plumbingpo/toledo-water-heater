Most tankless problems announce themselves the same way, with a blinking code on the display and a shower that turns cold. Behind that code is a system with very little margin for error. There is no storage tank to fall back on, so the heater has to detect flow, light a burner or power its elements, and raise the water temperature by fifty degrees or more in the few seconds it takes water to cross the heat exchanger. When scale narrows the passages, when the flow sensor stops reading, when the flame rod cannot confirm a flame, or when the vent or gas line cannot keep up, the unit protects itself by shutting down. That is the pattern we see again and again in Toledo, OH.

Error codes are a starting point, not a verdict, and we treat them that way on every Toledo call. The same ignition failure code can come from a worn igniter, a fouled flame rod, a gas line too small to feed the burner at full fire, or an air intake clogged with lint. That is why Toledo Water Heater tests before replacing anything. Our technicians read the fault history stored in the control board, watch flow sensor output with a faucet open, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, and put a manometer on the gas inlet to see what pressure actually reaches the valve when the burner is running flat out. We also compare inlet and outlet temperatures against the flow rate, which reveals a scaled heat exchanger long before it fails outright.

Once the cause is confirmed, most repairs are finished in a single visit. We descale heat exchangers with a pump and descaling solution circulated through the service valves, replace worn flow sensors, igniters, flame rods, thermistors, and combustion fans, clean inlet filter screens, and correct venting problems that trip pressure switches. When the real issue is a gas line sized for the old tank heater, we tell you plainly and quote the correct pipe run, because no amount of part swapping will fix a unit that is starved for fuel. If you are fighting a cold-water sandwich, we explain whether it is normal behavior for your model or a sign of a setting, fixture, or recirculation problem we can correct. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • A cold-water sandwich that has gotten noticeably worse
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet
  • Hot water that cannot keep up with more than one fixture
  • Repeated ignition attempts, then a shutdown
  • A fault code on the front panel and no hot water

Early Action Saves Money

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

From the outside a tankless heater looks like a plain box; inside, it is a tightly controlled system. One fault code often has several possible causes, and the sensors, igniters, and control boards involved are not cheap to buy on a hunch. Gas models add real hazards: the burner can fire at close to 200,000 BTU per hour, the vent carries combustion gases that contain carbon monoxide, and a loose gas connection or a vent joint that separates can put fuel or exhaust into the living space. Electric models bring a different hazard, often drawing well over 100 amps across several 240-volt circuits, so a loose lug or undersized wire can overheat quickly.

A trained technician with the right instruments gets to the answer faster. Ours read the fault history, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, test flow sensor output, and check gas pressure under full load with a manometer, which is the only reliable way to catch a gas line that holds pressure at idle but sags when the burner ramps up. We descale with a circulating pump and the right solution, then flush thoroughly so nothing corrosive is left in your plumbing. Just as important, you get an honest opinion: if the heat exchanger is damaged on an older unit, you will hear it from us before you spend money on a repair.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Flush scale out of the heat exchanger annually, and sooner in hard water
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Check the inlet filter screen periodically, since a clogged one starves the unit of flow
  • Tell us before adding a gas appliance, so the gas line can be checked for the extra load
  • If scale comes back quickly, add a softener or a scale-prevention cartridge on the cold inlet
  • Treat a code that returns after a reset as a symptom worth diagnosing, not ignoring
  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
