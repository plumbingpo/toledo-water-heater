The Water Heater Team Homeowners Rely On

Lukewarm showers, rusty or smelly hot water, popping and rumbling from the tank, a pilot that keeps going out: water heater problems tend to snowball once they start. Clear Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Nobody likes surprises on the final bill. That's why Toledo Water Heater quotes every job upfront, after a full diagnostic that covers the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the elements or burner, the anode rod and relief valve, and the vent and gas connections. You'll know the problem, what it takes to fix, and what it costs before you say yes.

The trouble with water heater failures is that most of them start inside the tank where you can't see them. Minerals settle into a crust on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod gets eaten away gradually, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. When hot water comes out rusty, the tank rumbles, or a small drip appears at the base, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

We also help homeowners plan ahead. Most tank water heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years, and a planned water heater replacement is always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency swap after the tank has already leaked. Our technicians know how hard water and local conditions affect tank life, so we can tell you honestly whether your heater has years left or is living on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing year-round. Regular service prevents failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still inexpensive to fix. At Toledo Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific equipment, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a temperature check at the tap.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. A tank in its later years needs a closer look than a new heater, a tankless unit on hard water calls for annual descaling to protect its heat exchanger working, and a large family works the tank far harder than a couple does. We build each visit around your heater, your water, and how your family uses it, so every service keeps the unit efficient and extends the life of the equipment you've already paid for.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule pays off. A clean tank heats water faster and more quietly, a healthy anode rod keeps rust from attacking the steel, and a working relief valve is ready to vent pressure if something goes wrong. Most families find that annual service costs far less than replacing a water heater even one year early.

Right-Sized Hot Water

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, the number of gallons of hot water it can deliver in a busy hour, while for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can sustain when the incoming water is cold. That's why we never just copy the gallon rating of the old unit. We look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

Picking a new water heater is about more than tank size. Our technician will compare tank models with tankless units, and gas with electric or heat pump, laying out operating costs, venting and gas line requirements, electrical service, and the installation itself. Tankless works well for many households, though it often requires a bigger gas line and new venting, and a well-sized tank can be the simpler, less expensive option. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

Homes in Toledo run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Toledo Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that never gets hot enough, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks that trip the breaker, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Not every water heater problem is equally urgent. A bad element just means lukewarm showers. A relief valve spitting water, on the other hand, signals that pressure or temperature inside the tank is too high, and a slow leak at the base can quietly ruin flooring before anyone notices. If you smell gas, get everyone outside and call the gas utility first. We come prepared to replace elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, add a thermal expansion tank when pressure is the culprit, and make leak repairs at fittings and drain valves.
