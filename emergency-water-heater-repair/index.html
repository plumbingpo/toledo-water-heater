How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Standing water around the water heater that returns after you wipe it up
  • Hot water far hotter than normal, a sign of a runaway thermostat or gas control
  • Violent rumbling or banging from the heater as it runs
  • Water or steam coming from the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge pipe
  • No hot water at all, even after waiting for the tank to recover
  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • Signs of scorching or backdrafting around the base or top of a gas heater
  • A breaker that trips every time the electric heater tries to run

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Emergency calls bring together electricity, gas, heat, pressure, and water, which is exactly the mix that makes do-it-yourself work risky. An electric heater sits on a 240-volt circuit that should never be handled while standing in water. A gas heater has fuel piping, a burner, and a vent that can spill combustion gases if something is out of place. The tank itself holds scalding water under pressure, and a discharging relief valve is signaling an unsafe condition that must be solved, not silenced. Capping that valve, relighting next to a gas odor, or forcing a rusted connection are all common and dangerous mistakes.

Why call a technician instead of improvising? Certainty and safe sequencing. We verify the shutoffs, check for gas leaks with proper tools, and confirm the area is safe for electrical work. Then we find the root cause. A discharging relief valve often means high street pressure or no room for thermal expansion; a puddle might be a drain valve, a dielectric fitting, condensation, or a failed tank; a cold house may trace back to a thermocouple, an element, or a tripped high-limit switch. Each one has a different fix. We repair what can honestly be repaired, and when the tank is the problem, we explain your replacement options without pressure.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Tag the water heater supply valve and the main shutoff so nobody has to search during a leak
  • Flush sediment from the tank yearly, since buildup overheats the bottom and speeds corrosion
  • Put the heater in a drain pan piped to a floor drain or the exterior where the setup allows
  • Keep boxes, paint, gasoline, and cleaning products well away from a gas water heater
  • A simple water sensor on the floor beside the tank can warn you hours earlier
  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
  • Set the temperature around 120F, which protects against burns and slows tank wear
